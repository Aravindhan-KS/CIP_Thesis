\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background of the Study}

Electric vehicles have become an important part of modern transportation due to their potential to reduce carbon emissions and dependence on fossil fuels. Lithium-ion batteries serve as the primary energy storage system in most electric vehicles because they provide high energy density, longer cycle life, and efficient charging characteristics. However, maintaining the safety and reliability of these batteries remains a significant challenge.

One of the major safety concerns in lithium-ion batteries is the formation of lithium dendrites. Dendrites are microscopic needle-like structures that can develop inside the battery during repeated charging and discharging cycles. Over time, these structures may grow and eventually cause internal short circuits, which can lead to battery degradation, thermal instability, or even failure.

Battery management systems are commonly used to monitor battery behavior through sensors that record parameters such as voltage, current, temperature, and capacity. These measurements form time-series data that can be analyzed to detect abnormal patterns. Traditional monitoring techniques rely on threshold-based rules or classical machine learning models. While these approaches are useful, they may not always capture complex nonlinear relationships that exist in battery sensor data.

Recent developments in quantum computing and quantum machine learning have introduced new techniques for representing and processing data in high-dimensional feature spaces. Quantum Support Vector Machines (QSVM) combine classical machine learning methods with quantum feature mapping to improve pattern recognition capabilities. This project explores the use of a hybrid classical and quantum learning framework to detect early signs of lithium dendrite formation in electric vehicle batteries.

\section{Problem Statement}

Lithium dendrite formation poses a serious threat to the safety and performance of electric vehicle batteries. Early detection of such degradation patterns is essential in order to prevent battery failure and ensure reliable operation.

Most existing battery monitoring systems rely on classical data analysis techniques that may not fully capture the complex interactions between battery parameters. As a result, subtle degradation patterns may remain undetected until the battery reaches a critical condition.

Therefore, there is a need for an intelligent monitoring system that can analyze multivariate battery sensor data and identify early signs of abnormal battery behavior. This project addresses this challenge by developing a hybrid quantum machine learning framework that applies quantum support vector machines to detect lithium dendrite risk in EV batteries.

\section{Objectives of the Project}

The main objectives of this project are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
\item To analyze battery sensor data including voltage, current, temperature, and capacity obtained from lithium-ion battery cycles.
\item To preprocess and extract meaningful statistical and electrochemical features from the battery dataset.
\item To apply dimensionality reduction techniques to prepare the features for quantum encoding.
\item To implement a quantum feature mapping approach using quantum circuits.
\item To develop a Quantum Support Vector Machine model for classifying battery degradation risk levels.
\item To evaluate the performance of the proposed model using appropriate classification metrics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope of the Work}

The scope of this project focuses on developing a hybrid quantum-classical machine learning framework for battery health monitoring. The system processes time-series battery data and transforms it into structured features that represent battery behavior over different operational cycles.

The study uses the NASA lithium-ion battery dataset for training and evaluation. Data preprocessing techniques such as timestamp alignment, missing value handling, and sliding window segmentation are applied to prepare the dataset for analysis. Statistical and electrochemical features are extracted from the processed data and used as inputs for the machine learning model.

The proposed system integrates classical feature engineering with quantum feature mapping and QSVM classification. The model predicts different battery risk levels, ranging from normal operation to critical degradation conditions.

\section{Motivation}

Battery safety is a critical requirement for the widespread adoption of electric vehicles. Failures caused by battery degradation can lead to costly damage and serious safety risks. Detecting early signs of battery deterioration allows preventive actions to be taken before critical failures occur.

Advances in machine learning provide powerful tools for analyzing large volumes of sensor data generated by battery systems. At the same time, quantum computing research has introduced new approaches that can potentially improve machine learning models by enabling richer feature representations.

The motivation behind this project is to explore the integration of quantum machine learning techniques with classical data analysis methods for battery monitoring applications.
